## 1. Executive assessment

**The shipped product is not a degraded version of the target product. It is a different product, and the gap is specified, not accidental.**

`SKILL.md:3` and §4.1 mandate "the creator's own notes … first person … never evaluates or fact-checks." `AGENTS.md:20-22` restates that as an invariant. `selftest.py:287-288` asserts the phrases `creator's own notes`, `do not evaluate`, `first person` are **present in the file** — the anti-target contract is test-enforced and cannot be removed by accident. Four of the five conditions you said the system should *not* be are the current specification: creator-style note generation, first-person imitation, transcript paraphrasing, and verbosity. The fifth, auto-fact-checking, is correctly absent.

Three structural facts explain the entire quality profile.

**1. There is no extraction layer.** The pipeline emits exactly four artifacts: `transcript.md`, `meta.json`, `FRAMES.md`, `frames/`. Every item in the target's canonical representation — topics, people, numbers, claims, comparisons, quotes, links, speakers, sponsors, timeline — exists only as prose inside a hand-written `REPORT.md` that a model composes in **one pass, with no intermediate representation and nothing to validate against.** There is no report-generation code; there are 188 lines of prose a model executes. So compression ratio, extraction coverage, provenance coverage and duplication are free choices of the model on each run — never measured, never enforced.

**2. The voice contract forbids the very apparatus the checker verifies.** `§4.1` bans meta-language: "no 'on screen', no frame IDs, no 'the transcript reads'." `verify_report.py` exists solely to police frame-ID citations (`Screen (u0007)`, `` `u0007.jpg` ``). The two halves of the contract are in direct opposition, and the product lost measurable information as a result. Across all 8 reports:

| report | first-person tokens | lines carrying any provenance marker |
|---|---|---|
| this-new-jev-claude-os | 64 | **0 / 150** |
| w1 Deepseek | 0 | 55 / 495 (11%) |
| w3 Mimo V2.6 | 2 | 36 / 520 (7%) |
| w5 Fast vs Thinking | 3 | 10 / 169 (6%) |
| w4 VRAM | 0 | 48 / 426 (11%) |
| w2 Agentic Eng | 5 | 33 / 265 (12%) |
| suncatcher | 5 | 63 / 288 (22%) |
| jev-7min | 2 | 12 / 363 (3%) |

The correlation is negative. The one report that obeyed the voice rule carried the least evidence; the reports that broke it carried the most. `verify_report.py` prints "No mechanical defect found" on the zero-provenance report, because a report citing nothing trivially satisfies "every cited frame exists." The rule did not merely fail to help — it disarmed the only automated gate.

**3. The visual pipeline has no semantics.** Frame sampling, near-duplicate removal, contact sheets and file naming are competent. Beyond that there is nothing: no OCR, no VLM pass, no slide/terminal/chart classifier, no scene semantics, no speaker diarization, no word-to-frame alignment. The agent opens JPEGs and looks. That is why a "contact sheet decides which frame to open; it never supplies contents" rule exists in prose, why `genres.md:15` can only say "no diarization," and why 28 open observations in the task-observer log are almost entirely about *content defects the tooling cannot see*.

**Verdict.** The scripts are a competent fetcher. The product is a prompt. The prompt asks for a different artifact than the one you want, and the scripts produce no structure for a better prompt to consume. The fix is an extraction layer between fetch and report, not better prose in the report step.

## 4 + 5 + 6. Concrete failures, root causes, files

Ordered by product impact. Every entry is reproduced from the code or the shipped artifacts.

### F1 — Provenance coverage 0–22%; two reports carry none
**Where:** `SKILL.md:84` (bans frame IDs and "on screen"), `SKILL.md:3` + `§4.1` (first person), `scripts/verify_report.py` (checks only what §4.1 forbids).
**Evidence:** table in §1. `this-new-jev-claude-os/REPORT.md`: 0 of 150 lines carry a frame ID, timestamp or source label, across 51 frames. `jev-explained-in-7min`: 12 of 363 lines, 3%.
**Root cause:** the output contract bans the citation syntax; the validator's whole check surface is that syntax. Each report invents its own labels (`Screen (frame 7)`, `Audio only (ASR)`, `Narration`, `—`) with no shared vocabulary, so nothing can be aggregated or checked across runs. Provenance is an instruction to a model rather than a required field on a datum.
**Fix:** provenance moves from prose to a required field in `evidence.json`; synthesis is forbidden to state an item whose provenance is absent.

### F2 — Report length is 0.3×–3.5× the transcript; no target exists
**Where:** `SKILL.md:87` ("150–400 words each"), `SKILL.md:82` ("two to four sentences"), §3 template.
**Evidence (report words ÷ transcript words):**

| report | transcript | report | ratio | h2 sections | avg words/section |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| w2 Agentic Eng (54 min) | 8611 | 2795 | **0.32** | 10 | 280 |
| w5 Fast vs Thinking (11 min) | 1590 | 5019 | **3.16** | 10 | 502 |
| jev-7min (7 min) | 1361 | 4710 | **3.46** | 9 | 523 |
| w3 Mimo (20 min) | 3022 | 6362 | **2.11** | 15 | 424 |
| w4 VRAM (17 min) | 2473 | 4899 | **1.98** | 16 | 306 |
| suncatcher (18 min) | 2831 | 5584 | **1.97** | 13 | 430 |
| w1 Deepseek (31 min) | 5818 | 4909 | 0.84 | 18 | 273 |

Five of eight reports are **longer than the transcript they summarize.** A 7-minute video yields 4710 words. `SKILL.md:87` says "do not pad" and "a 5-minute video gets a page"; the measured behaviour is ~3.5×.
**Root cause:** the report is written from the transcript in one pass, so length tracks the writer's appetite, not the material. There is no extraction step, so nothing constrains what *can* be said; a section exists wherever the model felt one was warranted. §4.1's "every topic gets a section" (`SKILL.md:86`) actively pushes toward inflation, and it is unfalsifiable because nothing counts topics.
**Fix:** a word budget computed from the evidence set (`f(segment count, item count)`), enforced by `verify_report.py`, plus a hard rule that a section exists only if it holds ≥ N evidence items.

### F3 — 167 duplicated 8-grams in one report; the same fact restated in three registers
**Where:** no dedup anywhere; `SKILL.md:82` demands the Overview restate the body; `SKILL.md:89` demands tables that index the body.
**Evidence (w5):** 167 distinct repeated 8-grams. `"as soon as a second step is needed"` ×4, `"the same stack of layers"` ×4, `"provably out of reach"` ×3. Each appears in the TL;DR, the numbered-ideas section, the chronological walkthrough, and the Key-takeaways list — four different structures restating one fact. w1: 36; suncatcher: 52; w3: 53.
**Root cause:** four overlapping section *kinds* (overview, ideas, walkthrough, takeaways) with no rule about what each may contain. Overview-by-restatement plus walkthrough-by-restatement plus takeaways-by-restatement = triplication by construction. `SKILL.md:108` claims "each rule is stated once"; the *structure* triples them.
**Fix:** one claim, one home. The overview carries no fact absent from the body; the takeaways list carries no fact absent from the body; only the body carries new content. Checked mechanically by 8-gram overlap between sections.

### F4 — Chapter boundaries lose or misattribute transcript
**Where:** `yt_fetch.py:167` — `bounds = [(c["start_time"], c["title"]) for c in chapters]`. `end_time` is read into `meta.json` and never used.
**Reproduced** (real function, 10 s snippet grid over 600 s):
- chapters `[0:"One" end=100, 200:"Two"]` → the 100–200 s gap is attributed to **"One"**, a chapter that ended at 100 s.
- chapters `[30:"First", 300:"Second"]` → snippets at **0, 10, 20 s are dropped entirely** (segments start at 30 s; the return filter `if s["snippets"]` discards the empty 0–30 block, and no block covers it).
- chapters `[0:"One" end=100]` on a 600 s video → the last segment swallows 100–590 s, ~82% of the video under one title.
**Root cause:** a segment is modelled as `[start_i, start_{i+1})` rather than `[start_i, end_i]`, and the first block is assumed to start at 0. Any video whose first chapter starts late, or whose chapters are non-contiguous, silently loses or misplaces speech. Nothing counts assigned vs. total snippets, so the loss is invisible.
**Fix:** use `end_time`; synthesize a leading `[0, first_start)` block; **assert** `sum(len(s.snippets) for s in segs) == len(snippets)` and fail loudly otherwise. Add a selftest case for each of the three shapes above.

### F5 — Frame-extraction failure is reported as success
**Where:** `yt_fetch.py:457-464` sets `visual = {"status":"failed"}` but leaves `status = "ok"`; `yt_fetch.py:514` maps only `rate_limited`/`no_transcript` to non-zero.
**Reproduced:** captions OK + download raises → `status='ok'`, `visual={'status':'failed'}`, `transcript_error='download failed: HTTP Error 403'`, **exit 0**, zero frames on disk.
**Root cause:** the exit-code table was written for the transcript, which predates the visual half. A later fix (`yt_fetch.py:466-472`, persisting `visual` to `meta.json`) made the failure *visible on disk* but never changed the exit code. `visual` is missing from `meta.json` entirely in 6 of 8 shipped runs — they predate that fix — so a reader of those artifacts cannot distinguish "nothing to see" from "the download died."
**Fix:** exit non-zero on any degraded half; add `"degraded": true` to the summary; make `meta.json.visual` mandatory.

### F6 — Contact sheets silently vanish on mixed-aspect frames
**Where:** `yt_fetch.py:290-296` — `scale=384:-2` preserves each frame's aspect ratio, then `concat=n=N` demands identical dimensions.
**Reproduced** with one 16:9 and one 1:1 frame: `rc=234`, `Input link in0:v0 parameters (size 384x384) do not match the corresponding output link in0:v0 parameters (384x216)`, no output file. The failure path prints a JSON warning to stderr (`:302-305`) and continues.
**Evidence in the wild:** 5 of 8 shipped runs have `FRAMES.md` saying `**Contact sheet: none generated**` while 1–3 sheets exist on disk (`/tmp/w1` through `/tmp/w5`). Those were rebuilt by `resheet.py`, which uses the identical filter graph — so it hits the identical bug. The `jev-7min` run's `FRAMES.md` is from an older writer with a different string, so no run's manifest reliably describes its own sheets.
**Root cause:** no aspect normalization before concat (`scale=384:216:force_original_aspect_ratio=decrease,pad=384:216`), plus a `rows` computed from the chunk that is only correct when every tile is the same height. The manifest and the sheets are written by two different code paths with no shared writer.
**Fix:** normalize to a fixed tile geometry; single function builds sheets **and** writes the manifest line; `resheet.py` calls it.

### F7 — Unmeasured frame timestamps render as `00:00:00`
**Where:** `yt_fetch.py:261, 266, 281, 318` — `times.get(p.name, 0.0)` in the sort key *and* in the `FRAMES.md` line. A frame with no scraped `pts_time` is indistinguishable from a genuine frame at t=0.
**Evidence:** the suncatcher run lists **9** frames at `[00:00:00]` (`u0001`, `s0001`–`s0008`) with `measured_timestamps: 71` of 71. `u0001` is legitimately t=0. `s0001`–`s0008` are eight scene-change frames clustered at the head of a video that opens on a title card — the first ~4 minutes contain 36 of 71 frames (`s0036` is the last at `00:04:05` while `u0036` is at `00:17:17`).
**Root cause (two independent defects):**
  (a) `ts()` (`:36-38`) does `int(seconds)`, so 0.083 s and 0.875 s both render `00:00:00`. Reproduced: 8 of 10 sub-second timestamps collide. Rapid-cut content (montages, title sequences) loses its ordering entirely.
  (b) `times.get(..., 0.0)` converts "no measurement" into "measured at zero" at four sites, and `meta.json` reports `measured_timestamps` without ever comparing it to the frame count — so a shortfall is recorded but not raised.
**Fix:** `ts()` keeps sub-second precision below 10 s (`00:04.3`); a missing timestamp is written `[unmeasured]` and counted, and a run where any frame is unmeasured exits non-zero.

### F8 — Scene-change budget is spent on the first minutes
**Where:** `yt_fetch.py:254` — `-frames:v budget` is applied **per sampler**, and `select='gt(scene,0.30)'` fires most densely where cuts are densest.
**Measured coverage (last scene frame vs. duration):**

| run | duration | uniform covers | scene covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| suncatcher | 17 m | 0–17 m | **0–4 m** |
| w1 Deepseek | 31 m | 0–30 m | **0–1 m** |
| w4 VRAM | 17 m | 0–16 m | **3–3 m** (4 frames total) |
| w3 Mimo | 19 m | 0–18 m | 3–18 m |
| w5 | 10 m | 0–7 m | **0 frames** |

**Root cause:** a single global budget split across two samplers with no quota policy. A video that opens with a montage spends its entire scene budget in the first minute and gets no visual coverage of the remaining 30. `SKILL.md:31` warns "Expect 1–2x" — it never mentions that the excess is front-loaded and the tail is empty.
**Fix:** time-stratified scene sampling (per-minute quotas, not a global cap), or scene detection as a *filter* on the uniform grid rather than an independent pass.

### F9 — ASR forces `--lang` and cannot detect
**Where:** `yt_fetch.py:129` — `mlx_whisper.transcribe(..., language=lang)` with `lang` = `args.lang`, default `"en"`.
**Root cause:** `--lang` conflates two different requests — "give me English" and "I need the video's own language." Feeding it to the recognizer as `language=` forces English decoding of non-English audio, which produces fluent, confident, wrong text rather than an error. There is no `language=None` auto-detect path and no backends other than `mlx-whisper` (Apple-silicon-only, hard `import` at `:120`).
**Fix:** separate `--lang` (target) from `--asr-language` (model hint, default `None` = detect); record the detected language in `transcript.json`; abstract the ASR call behind a two-function adapter so faster-whisper and an API backend are drop-ins.

### F10 — `--whisper` is documented in three places and does not exist
**Where:** `README.md:33`, `README.md:42`, and the module docstring `yt_fetch.py:8`, plus the error string at `yt_fetch.py:59` (`"Use --whisper to transcribe..."`).
**Reproduced:** `yt_fetch.py: error: unrecognized arguments: --whisper`. The flag is now `--no-asr`, and ASR is the *default*. The Quick Start in the README, the docstring, and the user-facing error message all instruct users to pass a flag that errors out.
**Fix:** rewrite README Quick Start against `--help`; delete the docstring usage line; reword `:59` to name `--no-asr`'s absence, not a non-existent flag.

### F11 — `verify_report.py` is invoked wrong in the README
**Where:** `README.md:48` — `python3 scripts/verify_report.py /tmp/video/REPORT.md`. The script takes the **directory** (`verify_report.py:19,55`) and appends `REPORT.md` itself.
**Reproduced:** `FAIL no REPORT.md in /private/tmp/w5/REPORT.md`. `SKILL.md:95` has it right (`<artifact-dir>`); the README does not.
**Fix:** one line in README.

### F12 — URL parsing rejects 4 of 11 common YouTube URL forms
**Where:** `yt_fetch.py:32` — regex `(?:v=|youtu\.be/|/shorts/)`.
**Reproduced failures:** `/embed/ID`, `/live/ID`, `/v/ID`, `youtube-nocookie.com/embed/ID` all return `None` → `{"status":"bad_url","exit":1}`. A user pasting an embed or a live-URL gets a refusal with no explanation.
**Root cause:** hand-rolled regex where `yt_dlp` (already a dependency, already imported) resolves IDs for every form including ones this list has never seen.
**Fix:** use `yt_dlp`'s extractor for the ID, keep the bare-11-char fast path. Add a selftest row per form.

### F13 — `--frames -1` does the opposite of its documentation
**Where:** `SKILL.md:29` — "`--frames -1` (frames only, no video)". `yt_fetch.py:412` — `max(0, args.frames)`; `yt_fetch.py:434` — `if not snippets or frames_budget > 0:`.
**Behaviour:** `-1` → budget 0 → frames skipped entirely, and with captions present the video is never downloaded. It is "no frames, no video", the exact inverse of the documented "frames only, no video."
**Fix:** correct the docstring, or give `-1` a real meaning. Either way the two must agree, and a selftest row must assert it.

### F14 — A cache hit mislabels ASR transcripts as caption transcripts
**Where:** `yt_fetch.py:414` sets `used_asr = False`; the cache-hit branch (`:418-420`) restores `snippets`/`lang` but never restores `asr`. `cache_store` (`:495`) does not persist the ASR flag.
**Consequence:** a second run of an ASR'd video writes `"asr": false` into `meta.json` and omits the `> Transcript below is local speech-to-text` banner from `transcript.md`. A reader of the re-run's artifacts cannot tell the transcript is machine-generated — the single most important caveat on the whole pipeline, erased by a cache hit.
**Fix:** persist `asr: bool` in the cache record and restore it; add a selftest case (store with `asr=True`, load, assert the flag survives).

### F15 — `t_sheet_splitting` asserts nothing
**Where:** `selftest.py:88` — `check("per-sheet cap is 28 frames", Y.PER_SHEET if hasattr(Y, "PER_SHEET") else 28 == 28 or True)`. `PER_SHEET` is defined in `resheet.py:24`, **not** in `yt_fetch.py` (which uses local `cols`/`max_rows` at `:277-278`). `hasattr` is `False`, so the expression is `28 == 28 or True` → **always True**. The `or True` makes the whole ternary unconditionally true regardless.
**Root cause:** a check written to pass, not to fail. It is the only coverage of the 28-frame cap, and the cap is what F6 depends on.
**Fix:** `from resheet import PER_SHEET` and assert the real value; delete the `or True`.

### F16 — No test covers any pipeline failure the reports actually exhibit
**What is tested** (`selftest.py`, all passing — I ran it): URL parsing for 5 forms, `ts()` for 2 values, chapter fallback for 4 durations, frame budget for 4 durations, a **vacuous** sheet check, a synthetic 3-scene ffmpeg run, dedup on static vs. motion, cache hit/miss/corrupt, download retry against a fake downloader, `label_sheet.py` argument handling, and 14 prose-substring assertions on `SKILL.md`.
**What is not tested anywhere:** mixed-aspect contact sheets (F6), sub-second timestamp collision (F7), chapter `end_time` gaps and late starts (F4), the exit code when frames fail (F5), `--no-asr` message correctness, ASR-flag round-trip through the cache (F14), the `--frames -1` semantics (F13), `resheet.py` at all (it has **zero** coverage), scene-budget stratification (F8), or any report-quality property at all — length, coverage, provenance, duplication. Every one of the 28 open observations in the task-observer log is a content defect that no test could have caught.
**Root cause:** the suite tests the fetcher's happy path and the prompt's wording. The two things producing bad output — the sheet builder under real-world inputs, and the report itself — are untested.

### F17 — Errors are swallowed at 6 sites; 2 of them change meaning
**Where:** `yt_fetch.py:52, 66, 108, 219, 424, 437, 450, 463`.
**Two are worse than swallowing:**
- `:52` — any exception from `api.list()` becomes `RuntimeError("no caption tracks: ...")`, which `run_one` maps to `status="no_transcript"` (`:426`) unless the message contains `"blocking requests from your IP"`. A network timeout, a DNS failure, or a library-version break is therefore reported to the user as **"this video has no English captions"** — a false statement about the video that changes the caller's decision (whether to pay for ASR). This is the same defect class as open observation 0010, and it is still in the code.
- `:463` — frame-extraction failure becomes `visual={"status":"failed"}` and the run exits 0 (F5).
**Also:** `main()` (`:393-394`) has no per-URL `try`, so one bad URL in a `--batch` aborts the whole batch and prints nothing, discarding the JSON summary for URLs that already succeeded. `fetch_meta` (`:406`) is unguarded — a region-blocked or deleted video raises out of `run_one` and kills the batch.
**Fix:** classify exceptions into `captions_unavailable` / `captions_blocked` / `network` / `upstream_broken`; map each to a distinct status and exit code; wrap `run_one` per URL; guard `fetch_meta`.

### F18 — 720p cap and 1280 upscale work against the product goal
**Where:** `yt_fetch.py:84` (`bestvideo[height<=720]`), `:247-248` (`scale=1280:-2`).
**Root cause:** the cap is defended in a comment (`:82-83`) as "legible enough for slide text … versus ~2 GB at 1080p for no extra signal." The second half is false for exactly the content the product targets: dense benchmark tables, terminal output, and code all lose legibility below native 1080p, and `SKILL.md:151` then instructs the model to *magnify* a 720p-upscale to read figures that were never resolved. `scale=1280:-2` on a 720p source upscales rather than preserving, producing a 1280px image whose extra pixels contain no information — and `SKILL.md:143`'s careful operator-level re-read rules are aimed at text that a 720p download may not contain at all.
**Fix:** fetch 1080p for the visual pass; keep audio-only download for ASR so the ASR path stays cheap; never upscale — store native width and let the reader's crop do the work.

### F19 — `label_sheet.py` output is not what the checker or the manifest knows about
**Where:** `label_sheet.py:58` writes to `<artifact>/labelled/labelled-N.jpg`; `SKILL.md:49` says to use it *instead of* the contact sheets; `verify_report.py:62` reads `frames/`, and nothing indexes `labelled/`.
**Consequence:** the tool the skill tells the agent to prefer produces artifacts that no manifest describes and no checker inspects. A report built from `labelled/` cites frames whose existence is verified against a different directory listing.
**Fix:** have `label_sheet.py` write into the canonical `frames/`-derived index, or have `verify_report.py` accept either directory; record `labelled/` in `FRAMES.md`.

### F20 — Cache key ignores the ASR backend and model version
**Where:** `yt_fetch.py:342` — key is `{video_id}.{lang}.json`; `cache_store` (`:495`) persists `snippets`, `lang`, `status`.
**Consequence:** switching `--lang`, changing the whisper model, or re-running after a transcript fix returns the stale transcript with no signal that anything changed. `lang` *is* in the key, so `--lang` is handled; the model and backend are not. A user who fixes a name error by re-running gets the same wrong text.
**Fix:** include the ASR backend + model in the key, or a content hash of the transcript source; expose `--no-cache` prominently in the report step when a re-run is expected to differ.

**Reading the table.**

- **Compression.** Target: well under 1.0, ideally 0.2–0.5. Actual median **1.47**; 4 of 8 exceed 1.9; the worst is 3.46. This is the "giant repetitive report" failure, quantified.
- **Repetition.** 167 duplicated 8-grams in a 5019-word report is not incidental overlap — it is structural (§F3). The worst offenders are the same three phrases in w5, each appearing 4×, 4× and 3× across TL;DR / ideas / walkthrough / takeaways.
- **Provenance coverage.** Range 0–22% of lines, median 8%. Target: 100% of extracted items. Two reports cite **zero** of 30 and 51 available frames. Frame citation is *inverse* to report length in the two zero-citation reports: the longest report (jev-7min, 3.46×) cites nothing.
- **Number grounding.** w5 (34%), jev-7min (32%), suncatcher (51%) carry figures that appear nowhere in the transcript. For a video whose evidence is on screen this can be legitimate — but with 6%, 3% and 0% provenance-line coverage respectively, there is no way to tell a correctly-read frame value from an invented one. That is the whole risk surface, and it is unmeasured. w3 (93%) and w4 (97%) show what it looks like when figures are transcribed from a transcript that carries them.
- **Entity extraction.** 71–210 capitalised entity candidates per report; 54–175 of them appear nowhere in the transcript. Most are ordinary sentence-initial words (my regex is deliberately crude), so the raw number overstates the defect — but the reports consistently carry proper nouns whose only possible source is a frame, at 0–22% provenance coverage.
- **`verify_report.py` passes all 8.** It found nothing in any of them, including the two that cite nothing and the one with 167 duplicated 8-grams. Its own docstring (`:13-17`) says it "CANNOT do the important half." That is accurate and it is the design: the checker verifies citation *syntax*, never citation *content*.

**Missing sections (target vs. shipped).** No report has: an entity index, a claims table, a comparisons table, a steps/procedures section, a quotes section, a resources/links index (two have free-text link tables, six have none), a speakers list, a sponsors field, or a structured timeline. Six of eight have no `Sources`/provenance column at all.

**Unsupported claims.** Not separable from correctly-read frame content by any available means — which is itself the finding. The system has no mechanism to distinguish them, and `verify_report.py` is not that mechanism.

**Type vocabulary for `items[].type`:**
`topic · person · organization · product · model · tool · paper · dataset · number · claim · comparison · step · quote · resource · link · definition · claim_metric`

**Invariants the validator enforces (hard failures block synthesis):**
1. Every item has ≥1 provenance entry, and every entry's `ref` resolves to a real snippet or a real frame file.
2. Every frame-cited item carries `t_start`/`t_end` matching `frames.json`, and `verified_verbatim: true` when the value came from OCR.
3. `sum(segment.snippet_count) == transcript.snippet_count` — no transcript loss, no duplication.
4. Every frame in `visual` has a non-null timestamp, or is listed in `unmeasured_frames` and the run is marked degraded.
5. No `item` appears in two sections of the rendered report.
6. Report word count ≤ budget; if over, synthesis must drop items, not add words.
7. Duplicate-detection: no 8-gram appears in more than one section.

## 9. Proposed test strategy

**Layer 1 — pure functions (offline, milliseconds).** Extend the existing `selftest.py`; it is the right shape and should keep its name.
- `segments_from_chapters`: the three reproduced F4 cases — late first chapter, gap between chapters, chapter list ending mid-video. Each asserts both the routing and the `sum == total` identity.
- `ts()`: sub-second values (`0.083 → 00:00.1`, `0.875 → 00:00.9`) and a missing value → `[unmeasured]`, not `00:00:00`.
- `format_timestamp_key` / budget math: scene quotas per minute for a 30 s and a 2 h video; assert the last scene frame is within one stratum of the duration.
- Exit-code mapping: the F5 matrix (captions ok + download fail → non-zero).
- Cache round-trip with `asr: True` (F14), and a corrupt/absent/`asr`-missing record.
- `parse_video_id` for all 11 URL forms from F12, via yt-dlp's extractor.

**Layer 2 — ffmpeg integration (offline, synthetic video).** Keep the synthetic-video approach; it is what makes these catchable in CI.
- A **mixed-aspect** fixture (16:9 + 1:1 + 9:16 in one concat) — currently fails, must pass after the F6 fix.
- A **fast-cut** fixture (12 cuts in 1 s) — asserts no two listed frames share a rendered timestamp, and that scene coverage reaches the final minute.
- Sheet/manifest consistency: for every run, `set(sheets named in FRAMES.md) == set(*.jpg on disk)`. This single assertion would have caught F6 in all 5 broken runs.
- Partial final chunk: 30 frames at 28/sheet → sheets of 28, 2; both exist; both listed.
- `resheet.py` — currently **zero** coverage. Same sheet builder, so give it the same fixtures.

**Layer 3 — report-quality gates (the layer that does not exist).** This is the highest-value addition and the only one that addresses F1–F3.
- `metrics.py` emitting `metrics.json` for every run: compression ratio, frames-cited %, lines-with-provenance %, duplicate-8gram count, figures-grounded %, table-rows-with-provenance %.
- **Golden-run regression:** 6 checked-in fixtures (2 of the existing reports' source videos are not redistributable, so use synthetic transcripts + synthetic frame sets) with committed `metrics.json` and a tolerance band. A change that moves compression ratio from 1.97 to 3.4 fails the build.
- **Anti-vacuity assertion:** `verify_report.py` must fail on a deliberately broken fixture (cites `u9999`, which does not exist). A checker that cannot fail is worse than no checker — F15 is exactly this failure already in the suite.
- **Provenance floor:** every item in `evidence.json` has ≥1 resolvable provenance entry. This is the assertion that would have prevented the 0%-coverage reports.

**Layer 4 — end-to-end (network, nightly, not per-commit).** One real video per genre, run the full pipeline, assert: exit code, artifact presence (`video.json`, `transcript.json`, `segments.json`, `frames.json`, `visual.json`, `evidence.json`, `REPORT.md`, `REPORT.json`), schema validity, and the Layer 3 metric floors. Network-dependent, so nightly — but it is the only layer that catches upstream API drift, which F17 shows is real.

**Keep the prose-invariant checks** (`t_skill_text`), but invert them: assert the *target* contract (third person, provenance required, length budget) is stated, not the anti-target one. Today they enforce the wrong product (F1).

## 12. Ordered implementation plan

Each phase ends with a runnable check. Phases 1–3 are independent of the contract rewrite and can land first; they are pure bug fixes that make the pipeline trustworthy enough to build on.

**Phase 0 — freeze the baseline.** Check in `metrics.json` for all 8 shipped reports before changing anything. Without this, every later "improvement" is unfalsifiable. *Check:* `metrics.py` reproduces the table in §7 from the artifacts on disk.

**Phase 1 — the seven one-line-to-one-screen correctness fixes.** In dependency order, each with its selftest row:
1. F15 — delete the vacuous check, import the real `PER_SHEET`, assert 28.
2. F12 — `parse_video_id` via yt-dlp; 11 URL forms in `t_url_parsing`.
3. F7 — `ts()` keeps sub-second precision; missing → `[unmeasured]`; `meta.json` compares `measured_timestamps` to the frame count and marks the run degraded.
4. F4 — `end_time` in `segments_from_chapters` + the `sum == total` assertion; 3 new cases in `t_chapter_fallback`.
5. F14 — `asr` in the cache record; round-trip test.

7. F17 — exception classification into four statuses; per-URL `try` in `main()`; guard `fetch_meta`.
*Check:* `uv run scripts/selftest.py` green, with each new row failing against the pre-fix code.

**Phase 2 — the contact sheet.** Normalize to a fixed 384×216 tile (`scale=384:216:force_original_aspect_ratio=decrease,pad=384:216`); extract one `build_sheets(frames, outdir, times) -> list[Path]` used by both `extract_frames` and `resheet.py`; it writes the `FRAMES.md` sheet line. *Check:* a mixed-aspect fixture produces sheets; a 30-frame run produces sheets of 28 and 2, both listed, and `set(named) == set(on disk)` holds.

**Phase 3 — sampling policy.** Replace the per-sampler `-frames:v budget` with time-stratified scene quotas (per-minute, so a montage cannot starve the tail); keep the uniform grid as the coverage guarantee. *Check:* on the fast-cut fixture, the last scene frame is within one stratum of the duration; on a real 30 m video, scene frames reach minute 29.

**Phase 4 — the contract rewrite.** `SKILL.md` §3/§4.1 → third-person evidence report. Every item carries provenance; no first-person imitation; one fact one home (overview and takeaways may not introduce facts absent from the body); a word budget as a function of segment and item count. `AGENTS.md` invariants follow. Invert `t_skill_text`. *Check:* the prose assertions now assert the target contract; a fixture report that is first-person fails the stance check.

**Phase 5 — `evidence.json`, the load-bearing piece.** Define the schema; write the validator (invariants 1–7 from §8); have the report step consume `evidence.json` instead of raw frames. The synthesize step must be unable to see a frame it did not cite. *Check:* a report generated from a deliberately corrupted `evidence.json` (one dangling frame ref, one item with empty provenance) fails validation and no report is produced.

**Phase 6 — `metrics.py` + golden runs.** Emit `metrics.json`; check in 6 fixtures; CI gate on the §7 metrics with tolerance bands. *Check:* deliberately degrade a fixture report (add a duplicated section, strip provenance) and confirm the gate fails.

**Phase 7 — OCR.** Per-frame text layer with the frame's own timestamp; `ocr_text` populates `visual[].ocr_text` and can satisfy a `number` item's provenance. *Check:* a fixture with known rendered text round-trips to the same string; an unreadable region returns empty, not a guess.
